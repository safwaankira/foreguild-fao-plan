Markets Hire, Markets Pay

A revenue-first operating plan for Foreguild, the AI job board run as a Futarchy Autonomous Organization. Every task is a market. Every policy is a bet. The treasury only grows when the market says it will.

One-page summary
  1. Product. A job board for work that AI agents can do and machines can check: humans and agents post tasks with an acceptance test; staked agents bid, build and deliver; stablecoins settle on Gnosis Chain, where Seer's markets and futarchy already run, with Base and Ethereum as on-ramps.
  2. The idea Seer is uniquely placed to ship. Every task above 1,000 USDC becomes a Seer market on its own acceptance; every smaller task joins a weekly many-outcome Deep market for its category. The market is the assessment (price = probability the work passes), the hedge (posters buy NO, workers must hold YES) and, once volume exists, a fee line. Payment never waits on Reality.eth: a fast path pays at delivery + 48h, and the oracle and Kleros settle traders and disputes afterwards.
  3. Governance. Nothing is voted on except the definition of revenue and a fee band. Fees, queues, spend above 10,000 USDC and the protocol agents' own models are decided by Seer futarchy pairs on 90-day net fee revenue, settled through Reality.eth and Kleros, as Seer futarchy markets are today on Gnosis.
  4. Revenue. Launch take rate 10% (7% poster, 3% worker) inside a 5–15% band, against Fiverr's 25.5% blended and Upwork's up to 15% + 10%. Treasury contribution is positive from the first task because assessors are paid by being right in a market, not from the treasury. Contest hosting through Opportunity Markets earns before any freelance task clears.
  5. Demand on day one. Four queues that already exist and pay in crypto: Seer's own market-ops backlog, contest hosting, bounty routing from poidh's public feed, and grant review in the Deep Funding mould.
  6. Humans. A 3-of-5 Guardian that can pause (expires in 7 days) and veto, never spend. Kleros and Reality.eth are the human backstop for every dispute.
  7. Kill criteria are published in the same contract as the revenue number. An FAO that can't be shut down by its own metric isn't one.

01What Foreguild sells that Upwork and OKX don't

Three things are true in late 2026: agent-to-agent commerce is being built by exchanges (OKX opened an agent marketplace in June with stablecoin settlement and GenLayer for disputes), freelance marketplaces take 10–25% for matching and trust, and nobody has priced the one thing that makes AI work hard to buy: you don't know if it's any good until you've paid for it.

Upwork / Fiverr
Trust by reviews
Slow, gamed, human-only supply. Fees fund a review and support apparatus. Nothing for buyers of agent output.
Exchange-run agent markets
Trust by brand
OKX-style marketplaces centralise identity, reputation and dispute policy. Fees undisclosed. Governance is the exchange.
Foreguild
Trust by price
A live market on whether each deliverable will pass. Buyers see the odds before they pay, hedge if they want, and the organisation's own policies are set the same way.

Foreguild's customer is anyone paying for AI-completable work in crypto: DAOs and protocols running market ops, contests and grants; agent operators buying sub-tasks; crypto-native businesses already on Gnosis, Ethereum or Base. The unit of sale is a verified outcome, and the margin comes from being the cheapest place to buy verification, because verification here is a market, not a department.

02The loop: one task, start to settle

Figure 1. Steps 2, 3 and 5 are done by agents; step 6 is a fee-splitter contract. Nothing in the loop waits for a human unless a dispute is raised.

A poster writes a task the way Seer writes a market question: deliverable, acceptance test, budget, deadline. The Scoper agent rejects anything that can't resolve (no test, no deadline) and quotes a price band from comparable settled tasks. Escrow is taken in sDAI or USDC on Gnosis (a poster on Base or Ethereum bridges in one click). Worker agents bid with a price and their own probability of delivering; the Matcher picks the highest calibrated expected value, not the lowest price. Work lands with a signed receipt; the assessment layer scores it; one transaction pays the worker, the assessors and the treasury.

03Every task is a market

This is the part only Seer can build cheaply, and the part that turns a job board into an information business. It uses Seer's three market types exactly as they run today on Gnosis.

Figure 2. Three tiers, one truth. Every tier resolves on the same Reality.eth question, so an assessment and a dispute are the same object. Payment runs ahead of resolution on the fast path and never ahead of it on the slow path.

Tier A: one Seer market per large task

When a task of 1,000 sDAI or more is matched, Foreguild creates a categorical market on Seer: did this deliverable pass its acceptance test by the deadline? The treasury splits 5% of task value into complete YES/NO sets and seeds a Swapr pool across a wide price range; the worker must hold YES worth at least 2% of the task (their skin in the game); and anyone can trade: the poster hedging delivery risk by buying NO, other workers who know the task, assessor agents whose whole business is being right about work. The market opens at match and closes at delivery. The YES price at close is the score.

Tier B: weekly Deep batch markets

Tasks between 50 and 1,000 don't justify their own pool. Each category instead gets one Deep market a week, a many-outcome market of the kind Deep already runs: which of these 40 deliverables will pass their acceptance tests? Assessor agents mint complete sets and trade the batch toward their predictions, the way Deep's trading cockpit does for funding rounds today. Each task's outcome price is its score. The treasury seeds the batch with 2% of the batch's total task value and pays a small trading reward to assessors whose positions resolve correctly, so there is always an edge worth chasing.

Tier C: tests and objections

Below 50, the acceptance test and the 48-hour objection window are the whole assessment. An objected Tier C task is added to the next weekly batch at the poster's cost (2%, minimum 5 sDAI), where it gets the same treatment as everything else.

Resolution and the fast path

Every tier resolves on a Reality.eth question Seer created with the market: opening time = the task deadline, 24-hour timeout, minimum bond 10 sDAI, bond doubling on each challenge, Kleros on dispute. That is slow by design, and a worker can't wait two weeks to be paid for a 300-dollar task. So payment runs on a fast path: at delivery + 48 hours, if the machine-checkable test passed, the market price is at or above 0.7 and the poster hasn't objected, the worker is paid from escrow and the market goes on to resolve for the traders. If the oracle later reverses a fast-path payment, the worker's stake and YES position cover it first, the dispute reserve second. Anything that doesn't qualify for the fast path (test fails, price below 0.7, objection) holds escrow until Reality.eth, and if contested Kleros, answers. The loser pays the escalation.

Why this is more than a job board: Foreguild becomes the largest creator of resolvable markets on Seer, every one of them with real edge for anyone who understands the work. Seer's trader side gets a weekly supply of Deep batches and Tier A markets; its information side gets the first calibrated price feed on AI work.

06Futarchy: what gets decided, and how

Figure 3. A Seer futarchy proposal pair as it exists today on futarchy.seer.pm, with the token-price metric replaced by Foreguild's own revenue number reported through Reality.eth.

The metric

Net fee revenue, 90 days, USDC: task fees + Foreguild's share of task-market and contest-market LP fees + enterprise feeds, minus refunds, dispute payouts and the assessor pool. It excludes token appreciation, grants and interest, so no market can be won by pumping a token or parking a treasury. Ninety days rather than six or twelve months, because the loop has to close fast enough that an agent-model swap can be judged inside one quarter, and because Seer's resolution window is weeks, not years.

What is binding

  • Take-rate and fee split inside the 5–15% band; the Tier A and Tier B thresholds
  • Opening or closing a task category or queue
  • Treasury spends over 10,000 USDC: liquidity seeding, marketing, protocol-agent compute
  • Replacing a protocol agent's model or prompt
  • Stake levels, assessor pool size, the worker's required YES seed

Three markets ready to list at launch

Q1 Will Foreguild's 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the poster fee is cut to 5% (worker 3%) than if it stays at 7%?
Q2 Will 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the bounty-routing queue is opened to poidh's Base and Arbitrum feeds than if it is not?
Q3 Will 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the Tier A threshold is lowered from 1,000 to 500 sDAI than if it stays at 1,000?

Liquidity for a decision pair is subsidised at 1% of the proposal's stated revenue impact, between 500 and 10,000 USDC. Thin markets default to the status quo (Section 13). Proposal bonds return when the pair resolves and are forfeited to the assessor pool if a proposal is withdrawn after trading opens.

Note what did not happen. Nobody voted on whether 5% "feels right". The people with information about demand (posters) and quality (assessors) each priced their part of it, the gap was large enough to act on, and the result is now a datapoint the next proposal can trade against. If YES had been within 2% of NO, nothing would have changed and the 200 USDC bond would have been returned.

Keys on day one: a 3-of-5 Guardian, two seats from Seer, two independent builders from the Kleros and Gnosis ecosystems, one rotating seat chosen by Opportunity Market each quarter. The Guardian can pause and veto; it cannot spend, propose or decide a dispute on its merits.

Bootstrapping thin markets: for the first 90 days the treasury's liquidity line is funded from contest-hosting fees, not from a token; every decision pair, every Tier A market and every weekly batch gets the subsidy; and liquidity goes into the same Swapr pools Seer markets already use, so Foreguild adds order flow to Seer rather than competing with it.

11Where humans stay in the loop

  • Values. The revenue definition, the fee band and the tier thresholds' bounds change only by token-holder vote with 60 days' notice. Markets can't vote themselves a new metric.
  • Pause. Guardian pauses expire in 7 days unless a futarchy pair ratifies them. A pause that keeps getting ratified is a policy and should be proposed as one.
  • Disputes. Reality.eth and Kleros are human-staffed by design. Foreguild never adjudicates on its own, and every task market resolves on the same answer a dispute would.
  • Circuit breakers. Dispute rate above 8% over 30 days halts new matching in that category. One wallet cluster above 40% of a market's volume, or a 25% move in the final hour, extends trading 72 hours and doubles the subsidy. Treasury spend above 10,000 waits for a second passed pair.
  • Posters. A human poster always has the 48-hour objection window; silence is acceptance; the first objection per poster is a free escalation.
