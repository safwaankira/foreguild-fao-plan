Markets Hire, Markets Pay

A revenue-first operating plan for Foreguild, the AI job board run as a Futarchy Autonomous Organization. Every task is a market. Every policy is a bet. The treasury only grows when the market says it will.

One-page summary
  1. Product. A job board for work that AI agents can do and machines can check: humans and agents post tasks with an acceptance test; staked agents bid, build and deliver; USDC settles on Base.
  2. The idea Seer is uniquely placed to ship. Every task above 250 USDC becomes a Seer market on its own acceptance. That one market is the assessment (price = probability the work passes), the insurance (posters hedge, workers signal confidence) and a second fee line (LP fees on every task). Small tasks use a lighter Deep-style forecast by three staked assessors.
  3. Governance. Nothing is voted on except the definition of revenue and a fee band. Fees, queues, spend above 10,000 USDC and the protocol agents' own models are decided by Seer futarchy pairs on 90-day net fee revenue, settled through Reality.eth and Kleros.
  4. Revenue. Launch take rate 10% (7% poster, 3% worker) inside a 5–15% band, against Fiverr's 25.5% blended and Upwork's up to 15% + 10%. Treasury contribution is positive from the first task because assessors are paid from their own slice. Contest hosting through Opportunity Markets earns before any freelance task clears.
  5. Demand on day one. Four queues that already exist and pay in crypto: Seer's own market-ops backlog, contest hosting, bounty routing from poidh's public feed, and grant review in the Deep Funding mould.
  6. Humans. A 3-of-5 Guardian that can pause (expires in 7 days) and veto, never spend. Kleros and Reality.eth are the human backstop for every dispute.
  7. Kill criteria are published in the same contract as the revenue number. An FAO that can't be shut down by its own metric isn't one.

01What Foreguild sells that Upwork and OKX don't

Three things are true in late 2026: agent-to-agent commerce is being built by exchanges (OKX opened an agent marketplace in June with stablecoin settlement and GenLayer for disputes), freelance marketplaces take 10–25% for matching and trust, and nobody has priced the one thing that makes AI work hard to buy: you don't know if it's any good until you've paid for it.

Upwork / Fiverr
Trust by reviews
Slow, gamed, human-only supply. Fees fund a review and support apparatus. Nothing for buyers of agent output.
Exchange-run agent markets
Trust by brand
OKX-style marketplaces centralise identity, reputation and dispute policy. Fees undisclosed. Governance is the exchange.
Foreguild
Trust by price
A live market on whether each deliverable will pass. Buyers see the odds before they pay, hedge if they want, and the organisation's own policies are set the same way.

Foreguild's customer is anyone paying for AI-completable work in crypto: DAOs and protocols running market ops, contests and grants; agent operators buying sub-tasks; crypto-native businesses that already live on Base. The unit of sale is a verified outcome, and the margin comes from being the cheapest place to buy verification, because verification here is a market, not a department.

02The loop: one task, start to settle

Figure 1. Steps 2, 3 and 5 are done by agents; step 6 is a fee-splitter contract. Nothing in the loop waits for a human unless a dispute is raised.

A poster writes a task the way Seer writes a market question: deliverable, acceptance test, budget, deadline. The Scoper agent rejects anything that can't resolve (no test, no deadline) and quotes a price band from comparable settled tasks. USDC goes into escrow. Worker agents bid with a price and their own probability of delivering; the Matcher picks the highest calibrated expected value, not the lowest price. Work lands with a signed receipt; the assessment layer scores it; one transaction pays the worker, the assessors and the treasury.

03Every task is a market

This is the part only Seer can build cheaply, and the part that turns a job board into an information business.

Figure 2. One market per task does four jobs. Below 250 USDC, three staked assessors submit Deep-style forecasts instead, scored by Brier against the final resolution.

When a task above 250 USDC is matched, Foreguild creates a categorical market on Seer: will this deliverable pass its acceptance test by the deadline? The worker is required to seed YES with 2% of the task value (their skin in the game), the treasury seeds liquidity at 1%, and anyone can trade: the poster hedging delivery risk, other workers who know the task, assessor agents whose whole business is being right about work.

At delivery the market closes and the YES price is the score. Above 0.7 the splitter pays. Below 0.3 the task rematches, the poster is refunded from escrow and the worker's YES position and stake are forfeited. In between, the dispute ladder runs: the Reality.eth question behind the market is answered with a bond; if contested, Kleros decides, and the market resolves on that answer. The assessment and the dispute are the same object, which is why there is no appeals department to fund.

Two things fall out for free. The market pays LP fees on every large task, which Foreguild shares with Seer; and every resolved market is a labelled datapoint on how calibrated each worker's bids are, which the Matcher consumes directly and which Risk Pricing can expose as category-level risk. Foreguild becomes the largest creator of resolvable markets on Seer, and Seer's trader side gets a steady supply of markets with real edge for anyone who understands the work.

Below 250 USDC a market isn't worth its gas and liquidity. Those tasks get the light path: three randomly assigned, staked assessor agents each submit P(pass) plus the output of any machine-checkable test, the median decides, and assessors are paid from the 3% pool by Brier score against what the task eventually resolved to. Same incentives, no order book.

06Futarchy: what gets decided, and how

Figure 3. A Seer futarchy proposal pair as it exists today on futarchy.seer.pm, with the scalar collateral replaced by Foreguild's own revenue oracle.

The metric

Net fee revenue, 90 days, USDC: task fees + Foreguild's share of task-market and contest-market LP fees + enterprise feeds, minus refunds, dispute payouts and the assessor pool. It excludes token appreciation, grants and interest, so no market can be won by pumping a token or parking a treasury. Ninety days rather than six or twelve months, because the loop has to close fast enough that an agent-model swap can be judged inside one quarter, and because Seer's resolution window is weeks, not years.

What is binding

  • Take-rate and fee split inside the 5–15% band; the 250 USDC task-market threshold
  • Opening or closing a task category or queue
  • Treasury spends over 10,000 USDC: liquidity seeding, marketing, protocol-agent compute
  • Replacing a protocol agent's model or prompt
  • Stake levels, assessor pool size, the worker's required YES seed

Three markets ready to list at launch

Q1 Will Foreguild's 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the poster fee is cut to 5% (worker 3%) than if it stays at 7%?
Q2 Will 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the bounty-routing queue is opened to poidh's Base and Arbitrum feeds than if it is not?
Q3 Will 90-day net fee revenue be higher if the task-market threshold is lowered from 250 to 100 USDC than if it stays at 250?

Liquidity for a decision pair is subsidised at 1% of the proposal's stated revenue impact, between 500 and 10,000 USDC. Thin markets default to the status quo (Section 13). Proposal bonds return when the pair resolves and are forfeited to the assessor pool if a proposal is withdrawn after trading opens.

Note what did not happen. Nobody voted on whether 5% "feels right". The people with information about demand (posters) and quality (assessors) each priced their part of it, the gap was large enough to act on, and the result is now a datapoint the next proposal can trade against. If YES had been within 2% of NO, nothing would have changed and the 200 USDC bond would have been returned.

Keys on day one: a 3-of-5 Guardian, two seats from Seer, two independent builders from the Kleros and Gnosis ecosystems, one rotating seat chosen by Opportunity Market each quarter. The Guardian can pause and veto; it cannot spend, propose or decide a dispute on its merits.

Bootstrapping thin markets: for the first 90 days the treasury's liquidity line is funded from contest-hosting fees, not from a token; every decision pair and every task market above 1,000 USDC gets the subsidy; and Seer's existing market-maker configuration is reused, so Foreguild adds order flow to Seer rather than competing with it for liquidity.

11Where humans stay in the loop

  • Values. The revenue definition, the fee band and the task-market threshold's bounds change only by token-holder vote with 60 days' notice. Markets can't vote themselves a new metric.
  • Pause. Guardian pauses expire in 7 days unless a futarchy pair ratifies them. A pause that keeps getting ratified is a policy and should be proposed as one.
  • Disputes. Reality.eth and Kleros are human-staffed by design. Foreguild never adjudicates on its own, and every task market resolves on the same answer a dispute would.
  • Circuit breakers. Dispute rate above 8% over 30 days halts new matching in that category. One wallet cluster above 40% of a market's volume, or a 25% move in the final hour, extends trading 72 hours and doubles the subsidy. Treasury spend above 10,000 USDC waits for a second passed pair.
  • Posters. A human poster always has the 48-hour objection window; silence is acceptance; the first objection per poster is a free escalation.
